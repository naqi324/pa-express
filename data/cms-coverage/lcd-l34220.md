## Why this LCD (fallback for knee MRI)

No active LCD or billing article in any MAC jurisdiction covers knee or other
lower-extremity MRI. The full lists of final LCDs (969) and articles (2236) were
checked on 2026-09-25. Knee MRI falls only under NCD 220.2, which has no
knee-specific clinical criteria. L34220 is the closest active MAC policy for
musculoskeletal advanced imaging with testable criteria. The former JF policy
L37281 "Lumbar MRI" retired on 10/23/2025 when v40 merged JE and JF.

## Conditions for coverage

Sections: "Coverage Indications, Limitations, and/or Medical Necessity" (CILMN)
and "Associated Information" (Documentation Requirements, Utilization Guidelines).
All of the following must be met:

1. **Order (LMRI-1, Documentation Requirements).** "An attending/treating
   physician's order is required for each test. The order must be properly signed
   and dated."
2. **Clinical findings documented (LMRI-2, Documentation Requirements; CILMN).**
   The clinical findings and relevant prior treatment that support the need for the
   MRI are in the MRI report or clinical record. The history and physical exam
   typically include muscle strength, limb circumference, reflexes, sensation,
   straight leg raise, and sitting knee extension tests.
3. **Indication (LMRI-3, CILMN) — at least ONE of:**
   1. A **"red flag"** on history and exam: major trauma; minor trauma in a
      potentially osteoporotic patient; history of cancer; fever; chills;
      unexplained weight loss; recent bacterial infection; IV drug abuse; immune
      suppression; pain that worsens when supine or at night; saddle anesthesia;
      recent onset of bladder dysfunction; clinically significant or progressive
      neurologic deficit in the lower extremity; unexpected laxity of the anal
      sphincter; perianal or perineal sensory loss; clinically significant motor
      weakness; other nerve root compromise.
   2. **No red flag**, symptoms for more than 1 month, and no response to "a
      reasonable trial of conservative management lasting at least four weeks."
4. **Used for decision-making (LMRI-4, CILMN; Documentation Requirements).** The
   MRI result will be used for medical decision-making. The referring physician's
   record supports a contemplated diagnosis or treatment change. For uncomplicated
   degenerative disc disease or herniated nucleus pulposus, surgery or "other
   aggressive treatment (e.g. intervertebral joint injection)" must be under
   consideration.
5. **No duplication (LMRI-5, CILMN; Utilization Guidelines).** The MRI does not
   duplicate other imaging (such as a spinal CT). Normally only one lumbar MRI is
   sufficient; a second one needs documentation that comparative results are
   needed for a more definitive treatment decision.
6. **Technical and safety (LMRI-6, CILMN; CMS National Coverage Policy).** FDA
   pre-market approved MRI unit, operated within the approved parameters. No listed
   contraindication: contrast allergy (when contrast is used); pregnancy at the
   discretion of the primary doctor; metallic clips on vascular aneurysms (not
   covered, per NCD 220.2).

## Limitations and non-coverage

- Not medically necessary for uncomplicated degenerative disc disease or herniated
  nucleus pulposus when surgery or other aggressive treatment is not under
  consideration.
- Not reasonable or necessary when the findings will not affect treatment choices.
- Spinal imaging is not generally necessary in the first month of symptoms unless a
  red flag is present.
- Investigational, not covered: blood flow measurement, spectroscopy, imaging of
  cortical bone and calcifications, spatial resolution of bone or calcifications.
- MRI abnormalities alone do not retrospectively validate the need for the test.
- No extra payment for multi-position (reclining and upright) MRI.
